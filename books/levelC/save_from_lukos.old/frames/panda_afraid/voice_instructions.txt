Use a pillowy, high, warm childlike voice—soft and rounded, sing-song—now shaken with fear; keep all edges gentle, but let the pitch wobble and breaths tremble. Start with a tiny gasp that breaks into “οἰμοι!” as a startled cry, letting the word rise and quiver like a sob. Follow with “ἐγώ φοβοῦμαι!” a touch faster and tighter, voice small and shaky, ending in a breathy, shrinking exhale; no extra words, just a frightened whimper of air after. Keep Greek vowels open and legato: let “οἰ” ring plaintively, and in “φοβοῦμαι” sustain the long “û” with a trembling fall-off, as if clinging to safety.
