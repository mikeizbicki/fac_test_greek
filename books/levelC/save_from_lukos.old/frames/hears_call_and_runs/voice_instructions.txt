Use a warm, moonlit, grandmotherly contralto pushed by danger—distant, breathless, and fiercely protective. Let a tremor of fear flutter at the start of each call, but keep a steady, loving core that urges a child to act now. Deliver the first plea as a panicked yet controlled call carrying from far away; repeat it with a notch more volume and desperation, almost breaking on “βοήθει.” Keep the timbre soft (never sharp or biting) while the heartbeat quickens, projecting as if across a yard with urgency and space in the voice. Pronunciation: “ὦ Ὑπερβρέφος, βοήθει ἡμῖν” = “oh HOO-per-VREH-fos, vo-EE-thay ee-MEEN.”
