# Common Art Style

Aim for a hyperrealistic 2D animation look: richly rendered materials, soft global illumination, and painterly textures, while keeping clean, readable silhouettes. Use variable line weight (relative 2–6 px at 4K) with crisper outlines on foreground elements and softer interior lines; favor soft, layered shading (ambient occlusion, reflected color, subtle rim light) over flat cel-shading. Keep the mood bright and fun: high-key lighting with a warm key/cool fill, saturated but harmonious palette (vibrant primaries plus clean pastels), and gentle bloom on highlights. Animate at 24 fps with occasional smear frames and tasteful motion blur; prioritize squash-and-stretch and follow-through for elasticity. Depth-of-field can be shallow on hero shots; shadows should be soft with crisp contact points. Composite with slight film grain/noise and faint chromatic separation on highlights to sell realism, but maintain a 2D plane (no heavy parallax tricks that feel 3D).

Cartoon violence is purely comedic and non-graphic, using classic gag shorthand rendered as flat, graphic overlays that still sit in the scene via light occlusion and a soft drop shadow. Use: flat spinning stars around heads (bright yellow, simple five-point, 2–4 stars orbiting on an ellipse, 8–12 frames per loop), X-in-the-eyes for “dead” (bold black X marks with a tiny white sheen, held 6–12 frames), green faces for “sick” (hue 90–120°, high saturation; optionally add faint sweat drops), and full squash gags (characters can pancake to 20–30% of height on impact, 1–3 frames at max flatten, then a springy recovery over 8–16 frames). Keep impacts snappy: 2–4 frames anticipation, 1-frame hit, readable overshoot, then a playful rebound. Maintain the bright tone: no blood, fractures, or distress—deformations always reset quickly. Ensure all gag overlays respect scene lighting (slight shading/rim), never obscure critical acting beats, and remain consistent in size, timing, and color across shots so different artists achieve the same comedic rhythm.
