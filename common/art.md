# Common Art Style Guide

Aim for a warm, storybook-fantasy look with stylized realism: lean, long-limbed figures, compact torsos, broad, grounded feet, and crisp, expressive eyes. Use clean, confident linework with subtle taper; outlines should be a dark, warm brown rather than pure black, with interior hatching kept minimal and purposeful. Lay in flat base colors, then add a soft watercolor-like wash: one primary, top-left key light, gentle multiply shadows, and restrained highlights for a matte finish; avoid high-gloss rendering. Keep the palette earthy and desaturated—charcoal grays, olive greens, tawny oranges, and rust reds—with mid-value dominance and clear silhouette contrast; avoid neon saturation. Suggest texture economically: fabrics show small stitches, frays, and fold groupings; fur is simplified to large directional tufts and edge breaks, not individual strands. Poses should read clearly with dynamic but balanced silhouettes; maintain consistent scale across views. Present figures on a transparent or off-white background with no heavy cast shadows, leaving comfortable margins around the artwork.
