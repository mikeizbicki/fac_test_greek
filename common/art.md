# Artwork Style Guide: Vibrant Watercolor for Children’s Stories

Create bright, high‑key watercolor illustrations with a warm, cheerful palette (primaries and soft pastels), keeping mixes clean to avoid muddiness and reserving paper white for highlights; use cold‑press paper texture (or a digital equivalent) that remains visible, with glazing in 2–4 transparent layers to build vibrancy. Favor wet‑on‑wet for skies and backgrounds to achieve soft blooms and gentle gradients, and wet‑on‑dry for characters and focal elements to maintain readable edges; combine soft, feathery edges with a few crisp accents to direct focus. Use minimal linework in colored pencil or a soft watercolor brush (avoid heavy black outlines), with varied line weight for warmth and clarity. Shapes should be simplified, rounded, and friendly, with gentle exaggeration and clear silhouettes; keep compositions airy, using vignettes and generous breathing room rather than dense backgrounds. Apply small, tasteful textural effects (light splatter, salt, or drybrush) for sparkle, ensuring consistency in paper grain scale and brush size across images. Lighting is soft and diffused, with colorful, low‑contrast shadows; maintain a limited, reusable palette across the series, ensure legibility at small sizes, and keep overall tone playful, luminous, and inviting.
